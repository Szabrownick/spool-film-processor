3. Speaker through the LM386 amplifier (on the BOOT button)

Step by step

  1. Solder the 8-pin socket to the perfboard. Insert the LM386 last.
  2. Pin 6 (VCC) → the board's 5V pin. Capacitor C3 47 µF: plus to pin 6, minus to GND, as close to the chip as possible.
  3. Pins 2 and 4 → GND.
  4. GPIO9 (the BOOT pin with 3.3 V) → C2 2.2 µF (plus towards GPIO9) → R5 10 kΩ (“1002”) → pin 3.
  5. R6 470 Ω (“4700”) between pin 3 and GND. R5 and R6 form a divider that reduces the 3.3 V signal to about 0.15 V, which the LM386 accepts without clipping.
  6. C6 10 µF between pin 1 (+) and pin 8 (−). Raises the gain from 20× to 200×.
  7. Pin 5 (OUT) → R7 100 Ω → red speaker wire. Black → GND. R7 has no polarity. (R7 replaces the former C4 100 µF capacitor.)
  8. C5 10 µF: plus to pin 7, minus to GND. Recommended at high gain, it lowers the noise.
  9. Insert the LM386 into the socket with the notch on the pin-1 side.

How it works

  • The firmware briefly outputs an audio signal of about 1.5–4 kHz on GPIO9. The LM386 amplifies it and drives the speaker.
  • C2 passes only the audio to the amplifier and blocks DC, so the ESP board still boots normally (GPIO9 must be high at power-on).
  • R7 100 Ω limits the speaker current. About 20 mA of DC flows through the coil (about 7 mW) – safe for the 14 Ω speaker and the amplifier. In this build a 100 µF capacitor in this position sounded too quiet, so R7 is used.
  • The BOOT button still works as START / PAUSE / NEXT; pressing it gives a quiet click.
  • RESET (labelled 复位) is just a hardware CPU restart.

4. Identifying parts

SMD resistor: the first 3 digits are the value, the fourth is the number of zeros. 470 + 1 zero = 4700 Ω = 4.7 kΩ; 100 + 1 zero = 1000 Ω = 1 kΩ. Polarity does not matter.

SMD can: black field on top = MINUS (−). On the base, the cut corners are on the plus side.

Through-hole capacitor (C1 1000 µF): light stripe with “−” = MINUS. After desoldering both legs are equal, so go by the stripe.

LM386: the notch or dot marks pin 1. Numbering runs down the left side (1–4) and back up the right side (5–8).

GPIO9 pin: with the board on USB, measure DC voltage between the BOOT pins and GND. The pin with ~3.3 V is GPIO9, the one with 0 V is ground. Unplug USB before soldering.

TMC2209 V985 (verified: answers at address 0). Pinout per the manufacturer (Tenstar). R2 goes to PDN (4th pin); USART (5th pin) stays free. Without 3.3 V on VIO the driver does not answer and pulls the PDN line low.

6. Assembly order

  1. Desolder the parts from the donor board and measure the resistors.
  2. ESP board on the PC's USB: flash the firmware (section 8). On first boot tap the logo and calibrate the screen.
  3. Speaker + LM386 (section 3): a melody plays at power-on and touching the screen gives a click.
  4. DS18B20 + R1 → Menu → Thermometer shows the temperature.
  5. Charger module + battery: check polarity and 5 V at the output.
  6. XL6009 on its own: feed 5 V and, without load, set 12.0 V with the potentiometer.
  7. TMC2209 without the motor: VIO (3.3 V), GND, VM (12 V), C1, PDN via R2 to GPIO21, and EN, MS1, MS2, STEP and DIR to GND. USART and CLK stay free. Check that nothing gets hot.
  8. Motor: connect and disconnect only with power off. Then Settings → Motor test.
  9. Finally switch SW1, seals (section 7) and mounting in the enclosure.
Power: the module delivers up to 4 A at 5 V. The motor at 700 mA (stealthChop) draws about 0.6–1 A from 5 V, the ESP with the display about 0.1 A and the LM386 about 0.1 A while beeping, so there is plenty of headroom. Stick a heatsink on the TMC2209.

7. Seals and mechanics

Shaft passage: the shaft has a D-flat along its whole length, so the seal runs on an epoxied 8×1.5 stainless sleeve (the epoxy fills the flat). Print the Ø16 bore at Ø15.8–15.9 mm, 5 mm deep, 0.5 mm entry chamfer. Behind the seal a chamber with a drain hole: if anything leaks past, it does not reach the motor bearing.

Shaft seal – rules

  • Lip spring towards the chemistry. Mounted the other way round it leaks.
  • Grease the lip and the sleeve with silicone grease. Rinse the area with water after each process: dried blix wears the lip.
  • Centre the housing on the motor's Ø22 mm boss (ring Ø22.1–22.2 mm). An extra bearing is then not required; optionally a 688-2RS in the same Ø16 bore.
  • The motor itself is not sealed (ZZ bearing, housing joints). Seal the joints thinly with silicone and the cable exit with adhesive heat-shrink.
  • Seal friction is about 0.02–0.05 Nm. After assembly check Settings → Motor test.

A. Over WiFi (no cable)

  1. On the device: Settings → WiFi update.
  2. Connect a phone or laptop to the FilmProcessor network, password film1234.
  3. Open http://192.168.4.1.
  4. Choose SpoolFilmProcessor_v2.2_update.bin and send it.
  5. The device restarts by itself.

B. Over USB – update

  1. Connect the board with a USB-C cable.
  2. Run flash_update.bat.
  3. Enter the port (Enter = COM3; check the number in Device Manager → Ports).
  4. Wait for “DONE”.

If the port does not connect: hold BOOT, press RESET, release BOOT and run it again.

C. New board – full flash

  1. Run flash_full.bat and confirm with Y.
  2. Or in Chrome or Edge open espressif.github.io/esptool-js, Connect, address 0x0, file SpoolFilmProcessor_v2.2_full.bin, Program.
  3. After boot tap the logo and calibrate the screen (3 crosses → DONE).

The flashed version is shown at the bottom right of the logo screen (“v2.2”) and in Settings → Info (with the build date). Source code: the SpoolFilmProcessor folder (Arduino IDE, esp32 package 3.x – see INSTRUCTIONS.md).

9. Usage – quick guide

Starting a process

  1. After power-on the device opens the last used program (back → list → process types).
  2. START screen: processor upright / lying, does the probe touch the chemistry, chemistry volume, pre-wash temperature → NEXT.
  3. On the first “POUR IN” screen the top shows roll x/y of chemistry usage and the bottom the estimated time of the whole process.
  4. START on the screen or the BOOT button (START / PAUSE / NEXT).

Probe not in chemistry

  • The room temperature is stored once, on NEXT.
  • During pre-wash and rinses the probe works as a tap-water thermometer: drop of ~1 °C within a few seconds = water poured out (tank cooling counted from then), rise = refilled, max, “steady”. Colours: blue < 38 °C, green 38–45 °C, red > 45 °C.
  • Beeps: too hot – fast and high, too cold – slow and low.
  • The chemistry temperature is computed by the thermal model of the tank (Paterson Multi Reel 3) and the time is extended accordingly.

Useful

  • Touch calibration: touch the screen while the logo is shown. It is saved only after DONE or BOOT; without confirmation within 20 s it starts over.
  • Rotate screen 180°: Settings → Rotate screen.
  • Chemistry usage: on the DONE screen set the number of rolls → SAVE & FINISH. Developer and blix times grow per the manufacturer table.
  • Rinse by water changes: step editor, “Water changes” field.
  • Upright tank – streaks at the bottom edge: Settings → ROLL agitation: intense start 30 s, burst every 30 s (4 reversals). Soft start protects the agitator. An empty reel at the bottom keeps the film away from the exhausted layer at the tank floor.
  • A pre-wash with 40–43 °C water is safe for the emulsion and warms the tank. Avoid water above 45 °C and big temperature jumps between baths.
